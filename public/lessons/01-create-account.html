Make your Claude account.

Cost · $0 Needed · an email Difficulty · gentle

Before anything else, you need a Claude account. There's a free tier — generous enough to try every example in this guide — so don't worry about money yet. We'll talk about plans in the next chapter.

What we're doing

Claude is made by a company called Anthropic. There are two places you'll meet Claude: a website called claude.ai (chat in your browser) and a tool called Claude Code (Claude living inside your terminal). We're starting with the website, because the account lives there.

Step by step

  1. Open a browser tab and go to claude.ai.
  2. Click Sign up. You can use a Google account, an Apple account, or an email + password — whichever you prefer.
  3. Confirm your email if it asks you to. (Check your spam folder if the message hasn't arrived after a couple of minutes.)
  4. You'll be asked for a name and a few preferences. Use whatever you want — none of these are permanent.
  5. That's it. You're in.
A nice habit Use the same email here that you use (or will use) for GitHub. It makes a later step smoother. If you don't have GitHub yet — that's fine, we'll set it up in Chapter 03.

Say hello

You should see a chat box. Type anything — really, anything. A few warmups people enjoy:

  • "Explain how email actually works, like I'm twelve."
  • "Write me a short, kind note to send to a friend I haven't talked to in a while."
  • "What's something interesting about the year I was born?"

Claude will reply. You can keep talking — it remembers the conversation. That's the whole game: ask, follow up, redirect, refine.

One thing to know: Claude will sometimes be wrong. That's true of every AI assistant alive today. The more important the answer, the more worth verifying it is — but for learning, brainstorming, and most everyday tasks, you'll be amazed how often it nails things.

You've done the hard part

The account is the doorway. Everything else in this guide builds on what you just did. When you're ready, the next chapter looks at plans — Free, Pro, and Max — and helps you pick honestly.
