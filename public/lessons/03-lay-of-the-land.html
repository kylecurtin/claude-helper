The lay of the land.

Cost · $0 Needed · curiosity Difficulty · gentle

You're about to hear three names a lot in the chapters ahead: Anthropic, GitHub, and Google Cloud. Each is a real company you'll have (or already have) an account with. This is a five-minute orientation so the rest of the guide doesn't feel like alphabet soup.

Anthropic — the people who make Claude

Anthropic is the AI safety company that builds Claude. They're the equivalent of OpenAI (makers of ChatGPT), but with a particularly strong focus on building AI that's helpful, honest, and harmless.

When you signed up at claude.ai in Chapter 01, you made an Anthropic account. You don't need to think about Anthropic much. You think about Claude. Anthropic is the company behind it.

GitHub — the place your code lives

GitHub is, simply, where code lives on the internet. Think of it like Google Drive, but for code. Owned by Microsoft, used by basically every software developer in the world.

A few words you'll need:

  • A repository (or "repo") is a folder for one project. It contains all the files, plus a complete history of every change ever made to it.
  • A public repository is visible to anyone in the world. They can read it. They can even copy it. (But they can't change it without your permission.)
  • A private repository is visible only to you and people you invite.

Why does this exist? Two reasons.

  1. History. Every change is tracked. You can always go back to last Tuesday, or two months ago, or before that last mistake.
  2. Collaboration. You can share a repo with anyone — including Claude — so they can read and contribute.

You'll make a GitHub account in the next chapter.

Google Cloud (GCP) — the building blocks of the internet

GCP is Google's set of services for hosting websites, storing data, and running programs in the cloud. Think of it as renting computers, by the second, from one of the largest companies on earth.

You probably don't need GCP. This guide doesn't require you to ever sign up. The reason it's named here is so that when you hear "I deployed my project to GCP" — you know what someone is talking about.

Fun fact: the site you're reading is hosted on GCP. It happens to be free at the scale we use it.

The quick recap

  • Anthropic: makes Claude.
  • GitHub: holds your projects and their full history.
  • Google Cloud (GCP): runs computers and websites; mostly out of scope here.
Try this now Open three browser tabs: anthropic.com, github.com, cloud.google.com. Look at the homepages for a minute. Get a sense of what kind of company each is. Then close them. The names will feel less mysterious from here on.

A test (kind to yourself, please)

Without scrolling up, can you explain in your own words:

  • What a repository is?
  • The difference between public and private?
  • What GCP does, even if you'll never use it?

If yes, you're set. If not, scroll back up — the words are right there, and reading them twice is exactly the right move.
